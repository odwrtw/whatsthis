# Parsing policies and limitations

whatsthis guesses metadata from release names; ambiguous names cannot establish every field reliably. See [README.md](README.md) for installation and usage.

- **Titles preserve identity.** Movie subtitles after hyphens, installment labels such as `Part 2`, version-like decimals such as `2.0`, and non-metadata parenthetical text are retained. Ordinary filename separators are still normalized; explicit edition suffixes such as `Directors Cut` are excluded from the base title. An unbracketed `3D` before technical metadata remains part of the title, as in `Saw.3D.1080p.x264-GROUP.mkv`. Explicit presentation brackets are metadata; a repeated 3D/layout tag can establish that a trailing title `3D` is presentation metadata too. A standalone ` - Movie` can be removed when technical metadata establishes its role.
- **One primary release group.** An explicit trailing credit (`x264-GROUP`, including `-[GROUP]`) takes precedence over a credible leading `[GROUP]`, then a contextual trailing attribution is used. Recognizable websites, eight-hex-digit checksums, language tags, quality labels and processing annotations are excluded. For example, `x264-GROUP[example.com]` returns `GROUP`, and `[Team] Show - 12 [720p] [A1B2C3D4]` returns `Team`. Punctuation inside credited identifiers is preserved. Unknown tags cannot always be distinguished from uploader/group names; a fallback is a guess, not proof of authorship.
- **Explicit metadata aliases.** Supported variants include `m720p`, `720pBrRip`, `DDP`/`DDP5`/`DD+`, `QAACx2` (AAC), `PERSIAN`, and contextual `ESubs`/`E-Subs` (English subtitles). A bare `264` is not enough to infer H.264, and channel notation alone does not establish an audio codec.
- **Conservative ambiguity handling.** `Temp.3 [Cap.310]` retains episode 310 rather than assuming compact S03E10 numbering. After a leading group and title, an enclosed recognized resolution such as `[1080]` or `(720)` is metadata, not an implicit episode number. Explicit episode markers and absolute numbering such as `S01E720` or ` - 720` retain their episode meaning. Metadata-only suffixes after an episode marker retain bare resolutions and standalone years without requiring another technical tag; ordinary episode-title words do not establish that context. Ambiguous `LAT.SUB`/`VOF` tags do not establish named languages. Earlier or repeated years are not indiscriminately removed from titles. Conflicting resolution tags retain the last recognized resolution.
- **Entry-point distinctions remain intentional.** `File("Show.S4-11.1080p.x264")` treats the shorthand as a multi-season collection; `Video` interprets it as season 4, episode 11. Extensionless ranges starting at E01 (such as `S01E01-08`) are season-pack heuristics in `File`, not evidence that every episode is present. `Video` retains the first episode number; there is no episode-list field.
- **Language identity is standardized, recognition is contextual.** Language results use BCP 47 tags via `golang.org/x/text/language`, but filenames are not parsed as arbitrary language tags. The accepted release vocabulary and title-boundary rules still apply: valid ISO codes can also be codecs, title words or group names. `CHS` and `CHT` retain different scripts (`zh-Hans`, `zh-Hant`); distinct tags still set `Multilingual`, even when they describe the same base language.
- **The metadata model is intentionally limited.** Air dates, episode part suffixes/lists, source, edition, alternate titles, bit depth, channels, HDR/3D layout and separate website/uploader or audio/subtitle roles have no dedicated fields. Date-shaped names do not establish season/episode numbers and may fall back to movies without explicit numbering. Unsupported extensions (such as `.divx`) do not imply a codec or MIME type. Container/MIME can identify an `.srt` subtitle asset while `Type` describes its associated media.

## Maintaining expectations

The JSON fixtures are semantic expectations, not output snapshots to regenerate after parser changes. Regression tests cover complete results and identity-preserving transformations (changing a checksum, appending a website, or changing technical tags must not replace an established group or truncate a title).
